Kolkwitz-Anstötz, Pascal

## Poster

Antisemitism remains a persistent challenge in contemporary societies, taking different forms across diverse social, religious, and political contexts. In Germany, debates about antisemitism have intensified in the aftermath of October 7, raising pressing questions about how antisemitic attitudes can be identified, measured, and distinguished from legitimate criticism of Israel. My doctoral research addresses these questions by examining the manifestations, prevalence, and determinants of antisemitism across social, religious, and political groups in Germany. At its core lies a central challenge: Understanding antisemitism requires more than identifying which groups express antisemitic attitudes and to what extent. It also requires examining what we actually measure, how observed differences should be interpreted, and which underlying beliefs and social conditions help explain them. Drawing on survey data collected before and after October 7, the research explores how different manifestations of antisemitism relate to one another, where the boundaries between antisemitism and legitimate criticism of Israel lie, and why antisemitic attitudes vary across societal contexts. Particular attention is paid to ideological beliefs, including religious fundamentalism and conspiratorial worldviews, and their role in shaping antisemitic attitudes and the justification of anti-Jewish violence. By integrating questions of measurement, interpretation, and explanation, the project seeks to advance a more differentiated understanding of contemporary antisemitism and the conditions under which it emerges and persists. The poster invites discussion of the broader research questions guiding my doctoral project, its findings to date, and the open questions and future research directions that emerge from this work.
