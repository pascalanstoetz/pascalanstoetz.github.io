<br>
I am a political scientist and researcher at [GESIS](https://www.gesis.org/en/home/) – Leibniz Institute for the Social Sciences. My research lies at the intersection of political sociology and political psychology, with a particular focus on political attitudes, intergroup relations, and prejudice in post-migrant societies.<br><br> I received my Master’s degree in Political Science from [Philipps University Marburg](https://www.uni-marburg.de/en) in 2017. I am currently pursuing my doctorate at the Faculty of Management, Economics and Social Sciences at the [University of Cologne](https://cgs.uni-koeln.de/en/). My PhD project, <i>Antisemitism Across Societal Groups in Germany: Manifestations, Prevalence, and Determinants</i>, examines how antisemitic attitudes are expressed, distributed, and shaped across different social groups in contemporary Germany.<br><br>

From 2017 to 2021, I worked at the Department of Political Science at Philipps University Marburg, first as a research assistant and, from 2019 onward, as a research associate at the Chair of Political Science Methods and Empirical Democracy Research. I joined GESIS in 2021, where I contributed to the BMFTR-funded research infrastructure project [DP-R&#124;EX](https://datenportal-rechtsextremismus.de/en/) in the Department of Data Services for the Social Sciences (DSS) until the project’s completion in August 2026. Since September 2026, I have been a researcher in the Survey Data Curation (SDC) department at GESIS.
